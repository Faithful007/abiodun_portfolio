Tunnel Fire Quantitative Risk Assessment (QRA) System: Clean Energy Vehicle Integration

Python · PyQt · FDS · Monte Carlo evacuation modelling

An application for quantitative risk assessment of road-tunnel fires. From a tunnel's geometry, traffic and ventilation data, it generates fire-simulation inputs, runs fire and evacuation scenarios across fire positions and conditions, and computes evacuee exposure (FED), fatalities, societal-risk F–N curves and risk indices against acceptance criteria.

It includes a lane-level traffic model — multinomial-logit lane choice, a Greenshields fundamental diagram and method-of-successive-averages equilibrium — and its traffic and risk calculations are validated against reference outputs of the legacy tool it replaces.

Major developments over the legacy system:
  • EV fire statistics: the legacy system applied one fire rate per vehicle class; EVs are now a distinct fleet share with a dedicated fire rate from Korean data.
  • Event-tree split: every fire scenario is partitioned into ICE and EV branches.
  • HF source term: FDS input generation adds battery-derived hydrogen fluoride release.
  • Dual-consequence evacuation: one simulation reports FED-only and FED+HF fatalities.
  • Backward compatibility: zero EV share reproduces legacy results.
  • An AI layer lets engineers query standards and results in natural language, with every recommendation passing through a human review gate.

Train Performance Simulation (TPS) System

Python · Rail operations modelling

A train-performance simulator that models a train's movement along a route from track geometry, gradients, speed limits, traction and braking characteristics. It computes run times, speed profiles and energy use, and supports headway and operational planning studies for urban and mainline rail.

A built-in optimisation layer searches driving strategies — acceleration, cruising, coasting and braking phases — to find speed profiles that minimise energy consumption within a target run time, letting operators trade journey time against traction energy and quantify the savings of eco-driving and timetable adjustments.

AgentOps Intelligence Platform

Python · LangGraph · Ollama · FastAPI · PostgreSQL · Valkey/Redis · Prometheus · Grafana · OpenTelemetry · Docker

A multi-agent system that takes a business request — “analyse the quarterly sales data and prepare a management report” — and completes it with six specialised agents instead of one large prompt. A supervisor plans the work, a research agent gathers context, an analyst computes figures with typed tools, a critic verifies them, a report agent writes the document, and an approval gate holds the release until a person signs it off.

The project is as much about the engineering around the agents as the agents themselves: the platform measures its own latency, token use and cost, blocks unsafe inputs and tool calls, keeps an audit trail, and reruns a labelled evaluation set whenever a prompt, model or tool changes. It runs entirely on open-source software and local open-weight models — no paid API.

Orchestration & tool use

Supervisor-planned routing, a bounded ReAct loop in the analyst, and Pydantic-typed function calling: a safe calculator, read-only SQL, dataset analysis, retrieval, report rendering and an external API.

Human-in-the-loop that survives restarts

The graph is checkpointed in PostgreSQL, so a task paused for approval in one process can be approved and completed in another. Every decision is written to an append-only audit trail.

Hallucination detection

Every figure in an analysis must be traceable to tool evidence; a judge model from a different model family then reviews the reasoning. Failures are sent back for revision or escalated to a person.

Evaluation & regression gate

A labelled task set scores routing, tool selection, grounding, retrieval, safety, latency and cost. The delivery pipeline refuses a change that makes quality or safety worse than the accepted baseline.

Responsible AI by construction

Prompt-injection screening on requests and retrieved documents, PII redaction, SQL parsed and restricted to a read-only role, and agents that can reach only their own tools.

GenAIOps

OpenTelemetry traces per agent, model and tool call; Prometheus metrics and a Grafana dashboard for latency, tokens, estimated cost, tool failures and approval rates; model routing by task complexity.

Teaching Statement

How I teach, why I teach that way, and what students can expect from a course I run.

Teaching philosophy

I teach computing and statistics as ways of reasoning about real problems, not as collections of syntax and formulas.

A student has learned something when they can face an unfamiliar problem, choose a method, defend the choice, and say what the result does and does not show. Producing an answer is the smaller part; the judgement around it is what lasts beyond the examination.

Two convictions follow from that. First, understanding is built by doing: students need to write the program, run the analysis and be wrong in a setting where being wrong is cheap. Second, every student in the room can get there. Over nine years at a polytechnic and three at an international campus I have taught first-time programmers and confident ones in the same class, and my job is to give each a route to the same standard.

01

Teaching Methodology

Each topic moves through three steps: I explain the idea, demonstrate it live, and then students practise it themselves. In programming classes that means writing code in front of the class, mistakes included, so students see how a problem is actually worked out and how errors are found.

Laboratory and practical sessions carry as much weight as lectures. Tasks start from a worked example and are progressively opened up until students are working without a template.

Live codingWorked examplesLab-centred
02

Curriculum & Course Development

I design a course backwards from what students should be able to do at the end. At Federal Polytechnic Idah I taught the programming sequence in C, C++, Java and Python, so that each language introduced a new way of thinking rather than repeating the last one.

At Ghent University Global Campus I built the statistics group projects for each cohort from scratch: the dataset, the research questions, the analysis brief and the grading rubric, arranged so that the difficulty rises from descriptive analysis to non-linear modelling.

Backward designLearning outcomesProgression
03

Student Assessment & Feedback

Students should know how they will be judged before they start. I publish the rubric with the assignment, and I grade reasoning and choice of method alongside the final result.

Feedback is written, specific and usable: what was done well, what the analysis cannot support, and what to change next time. I have set and marked examinations, graded practical work and project reports, and used office hours for the conversations that written comments cannot replace.

04

Project-Based Learning

Projects are where separate topics become one skill. My statistics students worked in groups for a full semester on real data, for example a liver-function dataset of 591 volunteers, and had to turn an open question into an analysis they could defend.

As a lecturer I supervised National Diploma and Higher National Diploma student projects from proposal to working software. Group work also teaches what individual assignments cannot: dividing a problem, reviewing each other's work and writing for a reader.

Industry Experience in the Classroom

I teach from problems I work on. Monte Carlo simulation is easier to grasp when the question is how many people can evacuate a tunnel fire; optimisation is easier when the goal is a train that arrives on time using less energy.

I also teach the working habits that industry expects and courses often leave out: version control, testing, code review, validating a model against reference results, and documenting work so that someone else can reproduce it.

Applied examplesEngineering practiceReproducibility
06

AI in Teaching & Learning

Students now have AI assistants beside them, and I would rather teach them to use these tools well than pretend otherwise. Building AI systems professionally has taught me where they fail, so I ask students to check an AI-generated answer the way an engineer would: against evidence, with tests, and with a clear account of what they verified themselves.

Research direction (concept)

From Single-Tunnel Risk to Network Resilience

A tunnel or rail section is one link in a network. This direction moves from assessing the risk of a single asset to choosing investments and response plans for a whole multimodal network: optimisation under uncertainty over disruption scenarios, with simulation models such as the QRA and TPS systems supplying event frequencies, running times and energy use.
